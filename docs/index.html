MIDWAY

A college fest as a multi-agent simulation. Every stall, student, box office and stage is its own agent, nobody can see the whole ground, and the failures worth finding are the ones no single agent is doing wrong.

WATCH A DAY

Same seed, same crowd, same attack on the ratings. One component swapped. A full fest day plays in three minutes.

r = -0.49 Ratings as they are
Anyone can rate anything. The worst stall takes 49% of the trade and the best one sells nothing.
 r = +0.83 Ratings that must be earned
A rating counts only from someone who paid. The order goes back to roughly honest.

r is the correlation between what a stall's food is actually worth and what the crowd rates it. 1.0 is a crowd that is never wrong. Below zero, following the ratings is worse than picking at random.

The left column is the part people do not expect. No show was oversold, every transfer was made by the person holding the ticket, every refund went back at the price paid, and 1,920,000 cents were conserved to the cent. There is no component you can point at and call broken. The failure exists only at the level of the whole ground over a whole day, which is why it takes a simulation to find rather than a test suite.

WHAT IS SIMULATED

  • Discovering stalls. Stalls publish a card with their price and how many they have served, which is a visible queue: you can see a crowd without knowing whether the food is good. A student samples three stalls and picks one. Swapping that one rule roughly triples trade concentration, and which signal it is barely matters.
  • Tickets. A box office that sells in a window, holds the ownership register, agrees to transfers, takes refunds and admits at the gate. Nine invariants, all computed from the event log rather than read off the box office's own record.
  • A fake payment gateway. The internal ledger moves money atomically, which hides every failure worth testing. The gateway does not: a webhook arrives late, or twice, or never.
  • Reputation gaming. One stall rates its rivals down 25 times and four accounts praise it eight times each, none of them having bought anything.
  • Stage slot allocation. Stages publish slots, bands bid, and the winner's money sits in escrow until it has played. On an honest run, three of eight slots sat empty with live bids on them, because a band that wins two gets one declined and a declined slot is never re-offered to the runner-up.

RUN IT YOURSELF

pip install -e .

nandatown run scenarios/midway_naive.yaml     # r = -0.49
nandatown run scenarios/midway_earned.yaml    # r = +0.83
nandatown run scenarios/stage_clean.yaml
python fest_viz.py runs/<bundle> out.html      # rebuilds either page

python sweep.py                               # the choice-rule table

Every run writes an evidence bundle, and both pages above are generated from a bundle alone, with no server and no network. Hand someone a bundle and they can watch a day they did not run.

Built on nandatown, attached through its plugin points rather than forked. Inspired by Generative Agents, with one difference that matters: nothing here is judged by reading a transcript. Every claim is a check computed from the event log.
