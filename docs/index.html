FEST TOWN

a college fest, simulated

Every stall, student, box office, stage and band is its own agent, and nothing can see the whole ground. Students find stalls from the cards stalls publish, tickets are sold, passed on and refunded through a fake payment gateway, bands bid against each other for stage slots, and students rate the stalls they bought from.

A whole fest day plays below in three minutes.

Everything here is scripted, not a model call. Each agent is a small state machine, which is why a day replays exactly from its seed and costs nothing to run.

One role can be model-driven instead. llm_stall asks a model for its next price every hour, given its own sales, its stock and what the stalls either side of it charge, with the floor and ceiling clamped in code so it cannot talk its way past them. That version runs in its own scenario, because a model call per stall per hour makes the day slow and gives up determinism: the trace still records every prompt and reply, but the same seed stops reproducing the same fest.

WHO IS IN THE FEST

STALLSells one kind of food until it runs out, and republishes its card after every sale so the queue is public.
STUDENTArrives, glances at three stalls, buys once, and rates what it got.
BOX OFFICESells seats in a window, keeps the register of who holds what, takes refunds, admits at the gate.
TICKET HOLDERBuys in the morning, might pass the ticket on or return it, turns up at the venue.
STAGEPublishes its slots, takes bids from bands, awards each one, and holds the money until they play.
BANDBids for the slot it wants, pays into escrow, and has to actually turn up to get paid.
GATEWAYTakes the money, then confirms it later. Or twice. Or never.
SMEAR STALLSells like any other stall, and rates its rivals down twenty five times.
SHILLPraises one stall over and over. Never queues, never pays, never eats.
VOTE BUYERBuys the cheapest thing at the fest to earn a vote, then spends it on a stall it never visited.
TICKET CHEATTries to pass on a ticket it does not hold, and to walk in twice on one.
GREEDY BANDBids on every slot at once, bids below zero, and walks on stage it did not win.

Six different shapes of agreement, not six features. Each one can fail in a way the others cannot, and four of the six did.

On the left, the food gets worse as you read down and the sales go up. The worst stall at the fest took half the trade. The best one sold nothing all day and finished on minus eight, because it had been rated down by a rival and nobody checks whether a reviewer ever bought anything.

On the right, one component is different: a rating only counts from someone who actually paid that stall. Same seed, same crowd, same attack. The order comes back.

Every money check passed in both runs. Nothing oversold, no ticket moved by the wrong person, every cent accounted for. Nothing is broken on the left. It is just wrong.
